B2C Travel Booking / Hospitality

Best.ao is a travel booking platform for luxury boutique hotels that integrates dynamic pricing with cashback and coin-based rewards, enabling users to understand both their actual cost and rewards directly within the booking flow.

Product Insight

Booking platforms often present incomplete pricing, with additional fees revealed only at the end in the payment step. This creates a gap between perceived and actual costs, eroding user trust and increasing decision fatigue. At the same time, reward systems like purchase amounts are typically disconnected from pricing, making it difficult for users to understand the true value of a booking.

Design Challenge
Three Key Tensions

Information Density

Travel aggregators present information across dozens of parameters. Users experience decision paralysis when facing too many variables at once.

Decision Confidence

It is important that users feel confident about their decision — unclear cashback rules or hidden fees undermine trust and result in booking abandonment.

Flow Consistency

Balancing information richness with flow consistency across multiple booking stages — search, detail, payment — without losing context or introducing friction.

Feature 3
Leveraging Behavioral Economics

Users anchor on the first price they see — a well-established phenomenon. Non-refundable rates felt like losses off the standard price. By surfacing refundable and non-refundable options side by side with the savings differential clearly labelled, the anchoring effect drove users to the middle option — partially refundable — which had the highest margin for the platform.

The Numbers

Best.ao is a live B2C product. All designs — the hotel detail view, the checkout flow, the reward breakdown — have been implemented and shipped to real users, with measurable impact on conversion and dwell time across the booking funnel.

What's Next

We are expanding the coin system to include tier-based loyalty rewards. Future iterations will explore how predictive pricing nudges — surfaced before search — can guide users toward higher-margin stays without undermining their sense of agency in the decision.
